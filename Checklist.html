Bring-Up Checklist

Gas Leak Detector · Phase 1 (MQ-2 + LCD + LED + Speaker) · SMS disabled
Work top to bottom. Each stage assumes the one before it passed. If something fails, stop there and fix it — don't push on, because a fault early on usually explains every symptom after it.
Before anything else: the board should be unplugged for Stage 0. Only plug in the USB cable when Stage 1 tells you to.

Stage 0 — Power off: eyes and multimeter

Catching a wiring mistake now costs a minute. Catching it after power costs a component.

  • USB cable is unplugged from the Arduino.
  • No stray wire strands or component legs bridging the + and − rails. Look along the whole length of both rails, not just near the Arduino.
  • Continuity test: + rail to − rail reads open. Multimeter on continuity/beep. If it beeps, you have a short — find it before powering up.
  • LED polarity: long leg (anode) goes toward D3 through the 220 Ω resistor, short leg to − rail.
  • The 220 Ω resistor is actually in series with the LED, not accidentally in parallel.
  • MQ-2: VCC→+ rail, GND→− rail, AO→A0. DO left unconnected. VCC and GND swapped on an MQ-2 gets the module hot fast. Double-check the silkscreen.
  • LCD: GND→− rail, VCC→+ rail, SDA→A4, SCL→A5. SDA/SCL swapped is the single most common reason the screen stays blank.
  • Arduino 5V→+ rail and GND→− rail are both connected.
  • SIM900A, wall adapter and capacitor are not connected to anything. Phase 1 only. D7 and D8 stay empty.
Speaker gate — answer this before powering on. A 4 Ω 3 W speaker wired straight from D4 to ground draws far more than the 40 mA an Uno pin can source, and can permanently kill the pin or the ATmega.

Tick whichever is true:
  • The speaker is driven through an NPN transistor (2N2222 / BC547) with a 1 kΩ base resistor to D4 — or — I swapped in a piezo buzzer, which is safe directly on the pin. no code change needed either way
If you can't tick that box, disconnect the speaker entirely and continue without it. Everything else in this checklist still works — you'll just have a silent alarm until the transistor arrives. That is far better than a dead board.

Stage 1 — Toolchain and upload

Getting a clean compile before you care about hardware behaviour.

  • Sketchbook location contains no non-ASCII characters. File → Preferences → Sketchbook location. Yours was OneDrive\ドキュメント\Arduino; the AVR compiler cannot open that path. Move it to something like C:\Arduino. otherwise: "LiquidCrystal_I2C.h: No such file or directory" even though it is installed
  • After moving the sketchbook, reinstall LiquidCrystal I2C (Frank de Brabander) so it lands in the new folder.
  • Opened GasLeakDetector/GasLeakDetector.ino.
  • #define ENABLE_SMS 0 — confirmed still 0.
  • Tools → Board → Arduino Uno selected, and the correct COM port.
  • Plug in the USB cable now. Verify — Sketch → Verify/Compile. Sketch uses ~6616 bytes (20%), globals 585 bytes (28%)
  • Upload succeeds with no error.
  • Serial Monitor open at 9600 baud.
If the numbers differ slightly from the expected flash/RAM figures, that's fine — a different library version shifts them. Wildly different (or SMS-sized, ~10136 bytes) means ENABLE_SMS got flipped.

Stage 2 — LCD comes alive

The display is the thing most likely to need a tweak. Sort it before judging anything else.

  • Backlight is on. No backlight at all usually means VCC/GND, not the I²C address.
  • Text is readable — not blank, not solid white blocks. Solid blocks or a totally faint screen: turn the contrast trimpot on the back of the I²C backpack with a small screwdriver until characters appear.
  • If still blank: changed LCD_ADDRESS from 0x27 to 0x3F and re-uploaded.
  • If still blank after both addresses: ran an I²C scanner sketch and used the address it reported.
Backlight on but permanently blank at both addresses is nearly always SDA and SCL swapped. Recheck A4/A5 before assuming the module is dead.

Stage 3 — Boot sequence

Press the Arduino reset button and watch. This whole sequence takes about 23 seconds.

  • Splash appears: GAS DETECTOR / with SMS.
  • LED flashes once during the splash. confirms D3 and the LED polarity are good
  • One short beep during the splash (skip if the speaker is disconnected). confirms D4 and the transistor/buzzer are good
  • Screen shows Warming sensor.. with a countdown from 20 that decrements once a second.
  • Countdown reaches 0, then System Ready appears briefly.
  • Main display appears: Gas:nnn on line 1, Status: SAFE on line 2.
  • MQ-2 feels slightly warm to the touch after a minute. Its internal heater is meant to run warm. Hot enough to hurt is wrong — unplug and check VCC/GND aren't swapped.
Boots straight into the splash again and again (a reset loop) points at the 5 V rail sagging — usually the speaker pulling too much current straight off the pin. Disconnect it and retry.

Stage 4 — Sensor baseline and threshold

This is the calibration step. The default 400 is a placeholder, not a correct value for your specific sensor and room.

  • Let the MQ-2 run for a solid 5–10 minutes in clean air before trusting any number. The 20-second countdown is only enough to boot. A cold MQ-2 reads high and drifts downward for several minutes.
  • Serial Monitor prints a line every second: Gas: nnn limit: 400 [safe].
  • Readings have settled — drifting by only a few counts, not tens.
  • Wrote down the steady clean-air baseline: __________
  • Set GAS_THRESHOLD to baseline + 150 to 200 and re-uploaded. Example: baseline 180 → threshold 350. If your baseline is already above 400, the stock value would alarm constantly.
  • After re-upload, status stays SAFE in clean air with no flickering.
Baseline above ~600 in genuinely clean air means the sensor hasn't finished burning in. A brand-new MQ-2 wants several hours powered on before it's stable. Set a workable threshold now, then re-check the baseline tomorrow and adjust.

Stage 5 — Alarm test

Proving the trip actually works.

Use an unlit butane lighter, in a ventilated room, away from flame or sparks. Hold the button down for a second or so near the sensor to release gas without igniting it. Do not test by creating an actual gas leak.
  • Reading on the LCD jumps well past the threshold within a second or two.
  • Line 2 changes to !! GAS LEAK !!.
  • LED blinks steadily, roughly four times a second.
  • Speaker alternates between two tones, 800 Hz and 1200 Hz (skip if disconnected).
  • Serial line switches to [ALARM].
  • Nothing resets or freezes during the alarm.

Stage 6 — Recovery and hysteresis

The alarm must clear cleanly, and must not chatter on the way out.

  • Move the lighter away and let the air clear.
  • Alarm clears only once the reading drops 40 counts below the threshold, not the instant it dips under it. Threshold 350 → clears at 310. That gap is GAS_HYSTERESIS.
  • On clearing: LED off, speaker silent, line 2 back to Status: SAFE.
  • No stuttering — the alarm does not flick on/off repeatedly as the reading passes back through the threshold. this is exactly what the hysteresis prevents; if it stutters, raise GAS_HYSTERESIS
  • Triggered it a second time to confirm it re-arms.

Stage 7 — Soak test

A detector that false-alarms overnight is worse than useless — you'll stop believing it.

  • Left running untouched for at least 30 minutes in clean air.
  • Zero false alarms in that window.
  • Reading is still in the same range it started at — no steady climb. A slow upward drift means it's still burning in; re-check the threshold later.
  • LCD has not filled with garbage characters. Garbage after a long run usually means a marginal 5 V rail or loose I²C jumpers.
  • Overnight run completed cleanly (optional but worth it).
All seven stages ticked? Phase 1 is done and your detector genuinely works. The SMS module is the only thing left.

Phase 2 gate — SMS blocked: no capacitor

Do not start this until every box above is ticked and the parts have arrived.

  • 1000 µF 16 V electrolytic capacitor in hand.
  • 5 V 2 A wall adapter in hand — not the HW-131, not the Arduino's 5 V pin.
  • Capacitor soldered across SIM900A VCC/GND, as close to the module as possible, striped leg to GND.
  • Adapter − tied to the breadboard − rail — common ground, non-negotiable for serial to work.
  • D7 → RXD through a 1 k/2 k divider or level shifter. D8 → TXD direct.
  • Set ENABLE_SMS to 1 and filled in ALERT_NUMBER in +63... international format.
  • Measured SIM900A VCC while it registers on the network — stays above 4.2 V.
  • NET LED settles into a slow blink (registered), not a fast one.
  • Test alarm delivers an actual SMS.
