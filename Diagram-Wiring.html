Current Build Stage — Phase 1 & Battery Expansion

The SIM900A is not being wired yet. It needs its dedicated 5 V 2 A supply and the 1000 µF bulk capacitor before it will work reliably, and the capacitor isn't on hand. The firmware has it switched off at #define ENABLE_SMS 0 — the sketch compiles without SoftwareSerial and never touches D7/D8.
Build and test this first: MQ-2 → A0, LCD 16×2 on A4/A5, LED on D3, speaker on D4. Everything on this list runs off the Arduino's own 5 V pin. Skip the wall adapter, skip the SIM900A section of the diagram below.

Powering SIM900A via Buck Converter: Two 18650 batteries in series (2S) output 7.4V nominal (8.4V max). A 5V 2A/3A Buck Step-Down Converter steps this down to 5.0V with very high efficiency (>85%). Connect the Buck OUT+ in parallel to the SIM900A 5V pin and the breadboard 5V rail (NEVER draw SIM900A power through the Arduino).

Physical Wiring Steps for 2S Battery & Percentage Sensing

  1. 2x 18650 Holder (7.4V/8.4V) → Buck Converter IN+ / IN−. Connect holder positive (8.4V) to IN+ and negative to IN−.
  2. Buck Converter OUT+ → Breadboard +5V Rail & SIM900A 5V. Provides clean 5V to Arduino, SIM900A, LCD, and MQ-2 in parallel.
  3. Buck Converter OUT− → Breadboard − Rail. Creates the common ground for serial communications and power.
  4. Battery Percentage Sensing → Arduino A1. Connect raw battery positive (6.4V–8.4V) through a 100kΩ / 47kΩ resistor divider. Connect the junction between the resistors to Arduino pin A1.
  5. Solder a 1000 µF 16 V electrolytic capacitor across SIM900A VCC and GND as close to the module as possible to smooth out transmit spikes.
Sanity check: Measure voltage across battery raw terminals (should read ~6.4V to 8.4V) and at A1 (should read ~2.0V to 2.68V). On the 5V rail, verify a solid 5.0V output.

Important Cautions

SIM900A power. Do not run the SIM900A off the Arduino's 5V pin or the HW-131. It draws up to 2 A in bursts and will brown-out both (random resets, LCD garbage, failed SMS). Power it directly from the Buck Converter 5V output fed by the 2S battery pack, and tie all the grounds together.
Speaker on D4. A 4 Ω 3 W speaker driven straight from a pin pulls far more than the 40 mA an Uno pin can supply. Drive it through an NPN transistor (2N2222/BC547) with a 1 kΩ base resistor, or swap in a small piezo buzzer. Direct connection can kill the pin.
LED resistor. Put a 220 Ω resistor in series with the LED anode.
Logic levels. SIM900A RXD is 3.3 V tolerant. Feed it from D7 through a 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider (or a level shifter).

Upload Checklist

  1. LiquidCrystal I2C v1.1.2 is already installed.
  2. Open GasLeakDetector/GasLeakDetector.ino.
  3. Leave ENABLE_SMS at 0 for now. ALERT_NUMBER can stay as the placeholder.
  4. If the LCD stays blank, change LCD_ADDRESS from 0x27 to 0x3F.
  5. Select Tools → Board → Arduino Uno and the right COM port.
  6. Upload, then open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud — it prints a reading every second.
  7. Watch the clean-air reading, then set GAS_THRESHOLD about 150–200 above it.
Testing tip: hold an unlit butane lighter near the sensor for a second — the reading should jump well past the threshold.
Known environment issue. Your sketchbook lives at OneDrive\ドキュメント\Arduino. The AVR compiler cannot open paths with non-ASCII characters, so builds fail with LiquidCrystal_I2C.h: No such file or directory even though the library is installed. Fix it in File → Preferences → Sketchbook location — point it at an all-ASCII folder such as C:\Arduino, then reinstall the library there.
